## The netlist as a graph

Nodes are gates, edges are nets. A CRC register feeds itself, so the graph has
a cycle — but every cycle in a synchronous design passes through a register, so
cutting the edges that land on a flip-flop pin leaves a DAG, the combinational
core, which `hal_viz dag` levels topologically and draws left to right
(`images/dag.svg`, plus the standalone `images/dag.html`; `run_all.sh`
regenerates it in step 1).

**3 levels**, 13 gates, 111 edges, 64 cut at a register. Level 0 is exactly the
eight flip-flops. Five of the eight cut edges that start at a gate go
register→register with no logic in between (`u7`→`u9`→`u11`→`u0`→`u2`→`u4`);
the other three each pass through one cell, and all three of those cells read
`u4`. Joined up, the eight registers close into a single ring with one signal
injected at three consecutive positions — the shape of an LFSR and of its taps,
from connectivity alone. The only structure outside the ring is the two-level
reduction `u6`→`u8` over all eight registers, which is the whole reason the
design has a level 2; the CRC's own next-state logic is one gate deep. 89 of
the 111 edges are constant tie-offs, drawn as one `0`/`1` circle per consuming
pin rather than a shared GND/VCC hub.

## Reproducing

Synthesis needs Quartus Prime Pro 26.1 and the Agilex 3 device support; the
analysis needs a built HAL and Graphviz. Inside the build container:

```bash
export HAL_BASE_PATH=/work/build HAL_PY_PATH=/work/build/lib
export PYTHONPATH=/work/build/lib
bash examples/agilex3_walkthroughs/10_crc8_checker/run_all.sh
```

`check.py` alone is the CI-sized smoke test.
